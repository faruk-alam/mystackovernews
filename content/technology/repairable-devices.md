For years, the thinnest phone or laptop won the showroom display. A growing group of designers is making a different promise: keep the device working, and make the parts that wear out easier to replace.

That shift shows up in practical details. Batteries held in with screws rather than glue, ports attached to small boards and repair guides written for ordinary owners all make a product less likely to be discarded over one failed component.

## Repairability can be designed in

Engineers say durable products do not have to feel bulky. A modular port or accessible battery can fit inside a slim design when service is considered early, rather than added after the hardware is finished.

Clear part numbers and years of software updates matter too. A replacement part is useful only if owners can find it, afford it and install it with confidence.

## A different measure of value

Upfront price is only one part of the calculation. Buyers increasingly ask how long a product will last and what it costs to keep it useful. For manufacturers, that creates a chance to build trust through long support commitments.
