When a company experiments with a four-day week, the calendar change is usually the easy part. The harder work is deciding what can be removed, which meetings need a smaller group and how teams will know whether customers are still getting a good response.

Companies that treat the trial as a redesign exercise often start by tracking outcomes instead of hours. They ask what work creates value, where approvals wait and which recurring tasks can be simplified.

## Make the experiment measurable

Before a pilot begins, teams can choose a few indicators such as on-time delivery, customer response and employee retention. A clear baseline makes it easier to tell whether a new schedule is helping or simply moving work into longer days.

Not every role has the same rhythm. Retail, care and service teams may need staggered coverage rather than the same day off for everyone.

## Keep listening after launch

Regular check-ins reveal where handoffs have become stressful or where clients notice a change. Flexibility to adjust the schedule keeps a promising pilot grounded in everyday realities.
