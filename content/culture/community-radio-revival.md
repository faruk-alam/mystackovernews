Community radio has always had a strength that larger networks can struggle to match: it knows which road is closed, which festival is returning and which local question needs a patient answer.

Today many stations are extending that service online. A live show can become a podcast episode, while short social clips help a new listener discover a conversation already familiar to the neighborhood.

## Keep the local voice at the center

Digital tools make distribution easier, but reporting still depends on relationships. Volunteer hosts and local journalists can bring context to stories that travel too slowly or too quietly for national coverage.

Stations that explain how to contribute and how a story was checked give listeners a reason to keep coming back.

## Reach more people without losing the room

Streaming can connect former residents and listeners who cannot tune in over the air. The goal is to let those new channels support the on-air community, not flatten every station into the same playlist.
